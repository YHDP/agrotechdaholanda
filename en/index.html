The nexus

Water, food and energy are one problem on the farm

Pumping water costs energy. Irrigation secures the harvest. Storing the harvest costs energy again. We work where the three overlap.

  • Solar pumping, no dieselWater and energy: the pump runs on the sun.
  • Metered irrigationWater and food: the crop gets what the soil asks for.
  • Solar cold roomFood and energy: the harvest stays cold with the sun first.

Smart solar irrigation

The pump runs on the sun. Irrigation follows the soil, not the calendar.

Sensors measure soil moisture and the weather, and the control box starts the pump at the right moment. Without diesel or grid power.

Solar cold room

Harvest, chill on the farm, and sell when the price is right.

A container that arrives ready by truck, with a cold room and a technical bay. The cold comes from the sun first; at night, the battery holds some hours, and the grid or the generator covers the rest.

  • From +10 °C to −5 °C
  • Arrives by truck and can be moved
  • Production in 6 to 8 weeks
  1. Sun: by day, the panels cool the room and charge the battery.
  2. Battery: at night, it holds the cold for some hours.
  3. Grid or generator: takes over by itself when the battery reaches its minimum.

Each product has its own temperature and humidity setting. The full table is in the tech sheet.

Frequently asked questions

What does Agrotech da Holanda do?

We represent Dutch Flow Technology's technology in Brazil, in two product lines: smart solar irrigation, which irrigates when the soil asks for it, and the solar cold room, which chills the harvest on the farm itself.

Do I need grid power?

For irrigation, no: the pump works by day, on the sun. In the solar cold room the sun comes first and the battery holds the cold for some hours of the night. Plan a connection to the grid or to a generator as backup for the night; it takes over by itself when the battery reaches its minimum.

Do you work with cooperatives?

Yes. The solar cold room arrives by truck and can be moved, so one container can serve several cooperative members, following the harvest calendar. See the three ways to own one.
