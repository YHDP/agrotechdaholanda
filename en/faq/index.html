How it works

How does a solar pump for irrigation work?

A solar pump for irrigation works with panels that feed a solar pump inverter, and the inverter drives the pump directly, with no diesel. The pump sits in the borehole (submersible) or at the river or pond (surface). Systems run from 8 to 60 m³/h at about 30 m of total head, with 3 to 12 panels of 720 W. In complete systems, the control box reads the sensors and starts the pump when the soil asks; on the 1 and 4 ha systems, sensors and control box are optional. See the system or which size fits your area.

Does a solar pump work on cloudy days? And is it worth it in the rainy season?

A solar pump works on sunlight: under cloud, the flow drops; at night, it stops. In complete systems, if rain has already wetted the soil, the control box does not start the pump, so in the rainy season it runs less. The diesel savings come from the months in which you irrigate. And annual rainfall has been falling in 4,747 of Brazil's 5,570 municipalities (source: Lobelia Earth, Brasil in a +2°C World, Brasil 2C with Instituto Itaúsa, portal.brasil2c.com; measured 1980 to 2015, screening level, no substitute for a local study). Find your municipality on the water-risk map (in Portuguese).

What pump size and how many panels do I need? What area does it cover?

Pump size and panel count come out of the design, which starts with eight answers: crop, area, plant density, water need, location, soil, water source and irrigation type. The reference systems run from 1 to 10 ha, with 3 to 12 panels of 720 W. Larger areas, up to 30 ha per pump, are sized case by case in the proposal. Drip or sprinkler lines can come from Dutch Flow Technology or a local supplier. Answering commits you to nothing: ask for the design or compare the reference systems.

How does the solar cold room work and what temperature does it reach?

The solar cold room is a 20 or 40 ft container with an insulated room inside, cooled by solar panels and a battery, that runs from +10 °C down to −5 °C. Holding −5 °C takes 8 to 10 hours of refrigeration a day; nothing below −5 °C is promised. Next to the room sits the technical bay with the batteries, the inverter and the condensing unit with the compressor. The room is smaller than the container; exact dimensions come with the proposal, and the refrigerant and the other details are in the tech sheet. How it gets through the night: sun, battery and backup.

What is the right temperature for each fruit, vegetable or fish in the cold room?

Each product has its own range in the solar cold room: leafy greens 0 to 2 °C with high humidity; orange 0 to 9 °C, keeping 8 to 12 weeks; papaya 7 to 10 °C; ripe mango and açaí around 10 °C; fish on ice −2 to −0.5 °C; cured onion around 0 °C with low humidity. The ranges come from Embrapa, FAO and UC Davis; the açaí figure is common practice. Relative humidity control is optional. See the settings per product.

How do I cut post-harvest losses of fruit and vegetables?

Much of the post-harvest loss comes from heat and time between the harvest and the buyer, and cold soon after harvest, on the farm itself, helps reduce it. In the right range, oranges keep 8 to 12 weeks and papaya 1 to 3 weeks. Cold extends shelf life but cannot repair produce that arrived damaged: harvesting and handling still count. The solar cold room arrives ready on a truck and can be moved; how a cooperative shares it is in the cold room for a cooperative. See the solar cold room.

Energy, night and the grid

Do I need a grid connection?

For irrigation, no: the solar pump works by day, on the sun, and needs no grid connection. The solar cold room also uses the sun first, but its installation includes a grid or generator connection as backup for the night. How that works hour by hour: the cold room at night. For irrigation, a grid-tie inverter is an option for when there is not enough sun (tech sheet).

Does a solar pump work at night?

No, in the basic system the solar pump does not run at night: it works on the sun and stops at dusk. In complete systems, the control box starts the pump in the sunlit hours, when the soil asks, and the design sizes the system for the water your crop needs. If your crop needs water at night, a battery and a grid-tie inverter exist as options, sized in the design. Ask for the design.

Does the solar cold room hold the cold all night?

We don't know yet: the solar cold room has not been tested in Brazil's climate. That is exactly what we want to measure in the first pilot, together with a farm that wants those numbers before anyone else. In practice, the solar array and the battery can be extended, and 200 mm insulation is an option, but no unit has been extended that way so far. Until the battery is enough, the grid or a generator takes over automatically. Be the first farm.

Does it cope with the heat of the Nordeste and Centro-Oeste? Can I get more hours of cold without sun?

With an extra investment in insulation, 200 mm panel instead of the standard 100 mm, the solar cold room holds the cold for more hours without sun; how many more depends on the night and the load. Heat outside is what eats the hours: the tech sheet calculates the worst case on a 40 °C night, with about 5 hours of cold on the battery, after which the grid or generator takes over on its own. The hotter the night, the fewer hours on the battery. If your nights are like that, say so when you ask for a proposal: the extra insulation goes into the design as an option. Connecting to the grid here: 50 Hz and 60 Hz.

Does 50 Hz equipment work on Brazil's 60 Hz grid? Can I connect to the grid or a generator?

You do not need to adapt 50 Hz equipment: the solar cold room's compressor comes in 50 Hz and in 60 Hz, and Brazil runs at 60 Hz. The 60 Hz version is an option: ask for it in the proposal to connect to the grid or a generator here. The compressor is 220 V single-phase; how it connects to your supply goes into the design (the form asks about energy on site). That grid or generator connection is part of the installation and is the backup once the battery runs down (the cold room at night). Ask for a proposal.

Cost, payback and financing

How much does solar irrigation or the solar cold room cost?

There is no price on the site: the price of solar irrigation and of the solar cold room comes in a detailed proposal, with no obligation. For irrigation it moves with area, water source, total head, crop and options; borehole, installation, freight and import are quoted separately, and payment is 50% at order and 50% before shipment. For the cold room it moves with size, options, freight, import and installation. The PDF tech sheet comes by e-mail, with no obligation. Ask for a proposal or see what you save on diesel (calculator, in Portuguese).

Is a solar pump worth it? How fast does it pay back?

On diesel savings alone, the solar irrigation system pays for itself in about 3 to 6 years: an estimate, not a promise. Deep borehole and a thirsty crop: faster; river nearby and little irrigation: slower. The calculator shows the whole method, with crop water depth from Embrapa and FAO, total head and S-10 diesel at R$ 6.97 per litre (ANP national average, July 2026), or uses your real diesel use if you enter it. Yield gains are not counted, so real payback tends to be faster. Each litre of pump diesel avoided saves 2.24 kg of fossil CO₂ (Brazilian diesel carries 15% biodiesel; ANP and IPCC factors). Do your own sums (in Portuguese), then ask for the design with your numbers.

I already get the night irrigation tariff on the grid. Is solar still worth it?

It can pay combined: the sun by day and the grid when there is not enough sun, with the grid-tie inverter as an option, and the design sizes the combination. The site's calculator compares with diesel; if you already irrigate on the grid, say so in the proposal request (the form asks about energy on site) and the design considers sun and grid together. See the calculator (in Portuguese) or ask for a proposal.

Is the equipment Inmetro-certified?

Not yet. Today the equipment has no Brazilian certification. Which certification applies to each component is still being mapped: for example, Inmetro for panels, inverters and batteries, and Anatel for the communication module. The first sale only happens once the required certifications are in place. See also what is already running.

Can I finance it through a bank, with rural credit?

It depends on the bank and the credit line, and so far no line has been confirmed for this equipment: ask your bank whether its line accepts imported equipment, because it is made in Thailand. What helps the credit conversation: on systems with a control box, and only with your permission, the bank can be given read access to the financed equipment's data (flow, energy, soil moisture and run hours). That supports the application, but the decision stays with the bank. In the first pilot, help finding financing is included. On who sees your data: your farm's data.

Does a cold room pay for a small farm or a cooperative?

It pays best when shared: a shared solar cold room does not sit idle, it goes by truck from one member to the next, following the harvest. For a single farm, the sums depend on volume and season. The 20 ft room is the size for producer groups, cooperatives and seasonal use; the 40 ft serves aggregators, packing houses and large cooperatives. Three ways to own one: a single farm, a cooperative sharing among members (by booking, paying per use or with a yearly schedule agreed before the season), or an aggregator collecting from several farms. A small room is still in design. See the three ways or ask for a proposal for your cooperative.

Buying, delivery, warranty and service

How long from order to installation?

After the order, solar irrigation takes 12 to 16 weeks to a Brazilian port; customs clearance, freight to the farm and installation come after that. The solar cold room takes 6 to 8 weeks of production after the order, shipped from Thailand; freight, import and installation are quoted per project, so that figure is the factory time only. Before the order comes the design: it starts with the form (municipality, crop, area and water source), and answering commits you to nothing. Then four phases: survey and design; supply and installation; integration; and finally pressure and flow tests with team training. Plan by season: ask for the proposal one season ahead, or see the four phases.

Is there a warranty? How long do the pump, panels and battery last?

Yes. For solar irrigation, the contractual warranty comes in writing, with a manual in Portuguese: 15 years on the panels, 5 years on the inverter and control box, 2 years on the pump, Dutch Flow Technology's global standard confirmed for Brazil; spare parts stay available for 10 years, and, with maintenance, the equipment lasts more than 15 years. For the solar cold room, the battery carries a 5-year warranty; the other warranties and the spare parts come in writing in the proposal. Who repairs it and where the parts are: service. Tech sheets: irrigation and cold room.

If it breaks, who repairs it? Are spare parts available in Brazil?

Today there is no service network of its own in Brazil: nothing is installed here yet. So who does the servicing, how fast, and where the spare parts are stocked must be in writing in the proposal before you buy; ask for it. What is already settled: for solar irrigation, the warranty is contractual and in writing (15 years panels, 5 inverter and control box, 2 pump) and spare parts stay available for 10 years. For the solar cold room, the warranties and parts come in writing in the proposal. Who makes it and who answers for it: the company. Ask for a proposal.

Do I need a water-use permit (outorga) to irrigate with a solar pump or borehole?

It depends on the use: taking water to irrigate depends on a water-use permit (outorga) from the managing authority, state or federal, except for uses the authority deems insignificant (Law 9,433/1997, art. 12, §1). Whether your use falls under that exception, only your state's authority or a consultant can confirm. The permit is the farmer's responsibility, and the water source is one of the eight design answers: that is why we help with the process from the first conversation. Start with the design, or see the water-risk map (in Portuguese) for your municipality.
