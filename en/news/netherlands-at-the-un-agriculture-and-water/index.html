At the UN, the Netherlands speaks about climate-smart agriculture and water

On 24 September, Prime Minister Rob Jetten spoke at the UN General Assembly about climate-smart agriculture and water management. Here is what he said, and what it has to do with a farm in Brazil.

Published on 25 September 2026

The passage on agriculture and water

On Thursday 24 September 2026, Rob Jetten, Prime Minister of the Netherlands, addressed the general debate of the 81st session of the United Nations General Assembly in New York. The speech covered climate, food security and cooperation between countries. In one short passage he spoke about agriculture and water:

He did not mention Brazil or any company. The passage is about what the Netherlands has done for decades with partners in other countries, and about wanting to learn from them in return.

Watch the passage

The official UN recording is on YouTube, in English. In it, the passage runs from 11:13 to 11:30; the video opens three seconds earlier, at 11:10.

What it means for a farm in Brazil

For a grower in Brazil, climate and water show up in the monthly bill and in the size of the harvest. In practice, three things matter.

Water. With less regular rain, irrigating at the right time is worth more than irrigating a lot. Smart solar irrigation switches on by soil moisture, not by a fixed schedule.

Energy. Where the pump runs on diesel, every hour of irrigation burns fuel. With a solar pump, the energy comes from panels on the farm itself. The payback calculator (in Portuguese) shows what diesel costs your irrigation today, with your own numbers.

Cold after the harvest. Without cold, part of the harvest is lost between the field and the sale, or gets sold in a hurry. The solar cold room cools on the farm, in a container that arrives ready. It is a new line from Dutch Flow Technology.
