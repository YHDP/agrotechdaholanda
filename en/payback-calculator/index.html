› How we calculate

The calculator adds up two things: the energy that solar irrigation saves you buying (diesel, petrol or the power bill) and the extra harvest the water brings. The title is always the potential payback of the two together.

Estimate from your crop

From the area, the crop and the water source, we estimate the pumping energy and convert it into litres of diesel:

  • Water volume: ha × depth(mm) × 10 (1 mm = 10 m³/ha)
  • Hydraulic energy: 0.002725 × volume × total head
  • Diesel or petrol: energy ÷ pump efficiency × engine consumption
  • Yearly saving: litres × fuel price

If your pump runs on the grid today

The same hydraulic energy passes through the efficiency of the pump and of the electric motor and becomes kilowatt-hours bought from the grid:

  • Energy bought: hydraulic energy ÷ (pump efficiency × motor efficiency)
  • Price per kWh: tariff × taxes × (1 − irrigation discount)
  • Yearly saving: kWh × price per kWh

The irrigation discount makes grid power much cheaper than diesel. So for a farm that already has the grid with the discount, the energy saving alone usually takes longer than the system's life to pay for it. There the reason for solar is a different one: irrigating where the distributor grants no new load, or where the grid does not reach.

If you enter your consumption

Under advanced options you can enter your actual consumption, in litres a year or in reais a year. The energy part of the calculation then uses that figure.

Extra harvest

Extra harvest per year: area × (yield with irrigation − yield today) × the price you receive. The three fields come filled in with researched references for each crop, and you can replace them with your own figures.

  • Area not irrigated today: the gain is the step from rainfed to irrigated. Since you do not pay for pumping yet, the energy line compares with irrigating on diesel, petrol or the grid.
  • Area already irrigated: the energy is a real saving, and the harvest gain is only what management by soil moisture adds. Only coffee has a published measurement for this; for the other crops the gain stays at zero.

Potential payback

System cost divided by the combined yearly gain (energy + harvest). It is an estimate: rain, prices and management change the result from one year to the next.

System cost, landed in Brazil with freight and import: about US$ 2,000 per hectare with river or reservoir water and US$ 2,450 per hectare with a well, at R$ 5.10 to the dollar. It is an estimate. The panel rack is included. The well, the reservoir and the installation are not.

Sources

  • Price of S-10 diesel and regular petrol: national average in ANP's weekly price survey (Levantamento de Preços de Combustíveis), week of 20 to 26 September 2026.
  • Pump set efficiency (0.60): the FAO manual on pumping for irrigation (Module 5, Savva and Frenken, 2001) gives about 0.69 for a good pump and up to 5% loss in the drive. On centre pivots in Brazil, field measurements found 59% to 63% (Lima et al., Revista Brasileira de Engenharia Agrícola e Ambiental, 2009).
  • Diesel engine consumption (0.28 L/kWh): FAO, Module 5, which uses 0.25 L/kWh as a rule of thumb.
  • Petrol engine consumption (0.50 L/kWh): FAO gives 25% to 30% efficiency for petrol engines, and far less for small ones in the field. We use 25% and Brazilian petrol, which contains 30% ethanol and has less energy per litre. It is an estimate: adjust it if you know the actual consumption.
  • Total head: 40 m for a well and 20 m for a river are the base for drip irrigation (FAO, Module 5). Pivots and sprinklers need more pressure, so their actual diesel use is higher.
  • Water need per crop: Embrapa and FAO. Cocoa: mean water deficit of 275 mm in the dry season on the Transamazônica (Morais, Revista Brasileira de Ciência do Solo, 1998), with 90% microsprinkler efficiency: 300 mm a year. Upland açaí: 120 litres per clump per day, 400 clumps per hectare, from August to November (Embrapa, Comunicado Técnico 317, 2019): 580 mm a year.
  • Yield with and without irrigation, always on the conservative side (half of a gain measured elsewhere, or the floor of a range). Cocoa: Bahia, 600 to 1,500 kg/ha (Siqueira 2018, in Silva, UFRB 2020), counted at half. Açaí: Embrapa, 10 to 12 t/ha irrigated, more than 50% above rainfed upland açaí (2025), at the floor. Coffee: Emater-MG (2018). Beans: CONAB, winter beans under pivot in Goiás. Maize and banana: half the gap between irrigated and rainfed farms in the IBGE Agricultural Census. Soybean and sugarcane: half the gain of one trial (Rio Grande do Sul; Jaú, SP). Coffee already irrigated: Embrapa Cerrados, +13 bags/ha with irrigation management, counted as about 10%. Rice, mango, grapes, melon and vegetables have no reference: fill in your own figures.
  • Price you receive: cocoa on the Pará market, R$ 17.00/kg (Notícias Agrícolas, 2 October 2026); coffee at the CEPEA quote of 2 October 2026; açaí at the Pará average price and the other crops at the national average price of the Produção Agrícola Municipal 2025 (IBGE).
  • Power tariff (R$ 0.82/kWh): median rural low-voltage tariff (subgroup B2, TE + TUSD, before taxes) of the 81 distributors in ANEEL's open data, read on 30 September 2026. Eight in ten distributors fall between R$ 0.73 and R$ 1.01.
  • Taxes: PIS and Cofins of about 5% (CPFL, 2026). ICMS exempt for rural producers in São Paulo (Sefaz-SP) and 5% for irrigation in Mato Grosso do Sul (Sefaz-MS). In other states, check your bill.
  • Irrigation and aquaculture discount: 60% in the South and Southeast and 67% in the North, Centre-West and Minas Gerais, on low voltage (Neoenergia), and 73% in the Northeast. It applies to up to 8.5 hours a day, outside 17:00 to 21:30, with a water-use permit (outorga) (Lei 10.438/2002, Resolução Normativa ANEEL 1.000/2021 and Portaria MME 137/2026).
  • Electric motor efficiency (0.90): a typical value for motors of 5 to 15 kW. It is an assumption; adjust it if you know your motor's.
  • Pivots on medium voltage (Group A) pay contracted demand and a time-of-use tariff. The calculator does not cover that case: use the average price per kWh on your bill.
  • CO₂ from petrol: 1.56 kg of fossil CO₂ per litre of regular petrol, counting only the fossil part (70%) and the IPCC factor (2006).
  • CO₂ from diesel: 2.24 kg of fossil CO₂ per litre of the diesel sold at the pump, which contains 15% biodiesel (B15). The sum uses the diesel density and heating value published by ANP (Anuário Estatístico 2023) and the IPCC emission factor (2006). Biodiesel does not count as a fossil emission.
The pre-filled values are reference averages and can be adjusted. The result is an estimate, not a quote. The exact sizing of the solar system comes with the detailed proposal, made for your area.
